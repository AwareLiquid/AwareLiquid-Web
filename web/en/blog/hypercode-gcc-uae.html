The AI coding assistant Dubai developers are actually using in 2026

Dubai's engineering teams are past the "should we use AI coding tools?" question. The real question in 2026 is which tool — and whether it assists you line by line, or takes a whole task and finishes it.

Most tools today are copilots. They autocomplete, explain errors, and help you edit step by step. You stay in the loop, and for pair-writing that's exactly right.

But a growing number of teams are hitting a different need: delegating entire blocks of work. A valuation memo. A contract review workflow. A multi-file feature that touches tests, edge cases, and a green build. That's not a copilot job — it's a job for an autonomous agent that works for hours until it's done.

Three kinds of AI coding tools

  1. Editor extensions (Cursor and similar) — autocomplete, Q&A, step-by-step edits. Best when you want to lead every line.
  2. Terminal tools (Claude Code and similar) — powerful, command-line driven, flexible, but they require technical depth and hands-on steering.
  3. Autonomous agent apps — you give one clear instruction; the agent decomposes the task, develops in parallel, writes tests, drives them green, and delivers. You come back hours later to a finished result.

How to choose

Ask three questions:

  1. Autonomy or assistance? If you want to lead every line, use an editor extension. If you want to hand off the whole task and return hours later, look at autonomous agents.
